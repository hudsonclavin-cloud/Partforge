# PartForge reference tables — provenance, licence, confidence and doctrine

> **Status, 2026-09-18.** This document was written while the tables still sat in a scratch
> directory, so its "Integration notes" describe the repository as it was then: the ten tables are
> now `data/tables/*.json`, `tools/db/embed.mjs` projects them into `index.html` between the
> DB-DATA markers, and everything its final section asked for has been done — the compact O-ring
> rows carry their tolerances and the per-dash Parker Table 4-2 diameters, drills carry the printed
> mm rather than a recomputed one, the object tables pass `grades`, `design_rules`, `field_sources`,
> `core_fields`, `families` and table-level `field_confidence`, every hint line prints its row's
> confidence and a disputed marker, and the three repo defects it names (the end-cap fixture's
> AS568-240 ID and radial groove, the doctrine's face-seal widths, the gland-less hint) are fixed.
> `tests/flight-db-embed.test.mjs` now re-derives the projection from these files and fails if the
> shipped literal drifts from them. The rest of the document stands as written.

Written 2026-09-17 from the ten reconciled tables in `scratchpad/db/tables/*.final.json` (1,958,584 bytes in total). Every number in these files was compiled from memory of a standard, a vendor catalogue or an open-source data file, reviewed by three independent skeptic passes, and — where a host on `raw.githubusercontent.com` carried a usable copy — cross-checked by script. **No standard was read from the standards body, and no vendor site was reachable.** "certain" therefore appears only on values fixed by definition or arithmetic (pitches, thread-profile formulas, drill diameters, inch-to-mm conversions, rod diameters, ASME B36.10M pipe dimensions). "likely" means the value matched at least one independent published copy or three independent recalls. "recall" means one memory, or a value two reviewers disagreed on. The tables never inflate: where a reviewer disputed a cell the row carries a `disputed` field and the row label drops to the lower confidence.

Confidence at row level is always the weakest load-bearing field in that row; per-field detail is in `field_confidence` (fasteners_metric, fasteners_un, drills, rails), `field_confidence` at table level (npt), `hole_confidence` / `outline_confidence` (avionics), `tolerance_confidence` (stock), or the per-item `confidence` inside `supplement` arrays (orings, motors, airframes).

Rounding is NOT uniform across the set and this matters for joins: `drills` rounds inch-to-mm ties half-to-even to match the ASME printed column (3/16 in = 4.762 mm); `fasteners_metric`, `fasteners_un`, `npt`, `stock` and `rails` round half-up (4.763 mm). Join on the drill *name* (`#7`, `F`, `27/64`, `4.2 mm`), never on a mm value.

## 1. fasteners_metric — ISO metric coarse-thread fasteners, M2–M20

**File** `tables/fasteners_metric.final.json` — 58,241 bytes, 12 rows, 51 numeric/text columns plus `grades`, `design_rules`, `field_sources`, `core_fields`.

**Sources.** ISO 261:1998 / ISO 262:1998 (pitches); ISO 724:1993 (d3, D1); ISO 273:1979 (clearance holes, fine/medium/coarse); ISO 4762:2004 (SHCS dk plain and knurled, k, s, b); ISO 10642:2019 (countersunk, recall); ISO 7380-1:2011 (button head, recall); ISO 4032:2012 (hex nut s, e min, m max/min); ISO 4035:2012 / DIN 439 (thin nuts, recall); DIN 985 / ISO 7040:2012 (nyloc, recall); DIN 934 (AF differences at M10/M12/M14); DIN 974-1:2008 (counterbores, recall); ISO 7089:2000 (washers); ISO 898-1:2013 Table 3 / Annex A (As,nom, property classes); ISO 3506-1:2020 Table 4 (A2-50/70/80); ASME B94.11M-1993 (US drill equivalents); Machinery's Handbook / FED-STD-H28 (percent-of-thread and strip-shear formulas); VDI 2230 / ISO 16047 (torque order of magnitude); trade practice for tap drills (d − p, d − 0.85p); partial external confirmation of M2–M8 SHCS heads and tap drills against nophead/NopSCADlib `vitamins/screws.scad` (GPL-3 library; only numbers were compared, nothing copied).

**Licence / attribution.** Standards-fixed dimensional facts; not copyrightable. The standards documents are copyright ISO, DIN and ASME and are not reproduced. Attribution text the app must carry:

> Metric fastener dimensions per ISO 261/262, 724, 273, 4762, 10642, 7380-1, 4032, 4035, 7040, 7089, 898-1 and 3506-1, DIN 934/985/974-1, US drill equivalents per ASME B94.11M-1993. Values compiled from memory of those standards and reviewed, not transcribed from the documents; tap-drill, counterbore-depth, engagement and torque figures are trade practice and design rules, not standard values.

**Overall confidence:** likely (all 12 rows). No row is certain; per-field "certain" only on pitch, d3, D1, sharp across-corners, inch-to-mm conversions and fractional drills.

**Recall / disputed.** No row is recall. Disputed cells: `M6.nut_h_min_mm` (4.9 kept vs 4.7), `M8.nut_h_min_mm` (6.44 vs 6.14). Fields that are recall on every row where present: `cbore_d_mm` (12 rows), `jam_nut_h_mm` (12), `locknut_h_mm` (10), `csk_head_d_mm` / `csk_head_h_mm` / `csk_hex_key_mm` (10), `button_head_d_mm` / `button_head_h_mm` / `button_hex_key_mm` (8), `sti_insert_drill_mm` (10; null at M2/M2.5), `nut_h_min_mm` (7 rows: M2–M8). `grades.A2-70.disputed.size_range` (≤ M24 adopted; whether ISO 3506-1:2020 extends it is unverified). `torque_guide_8_8_dry_Nm` and `min_engagement_6061T6_8_8_mm` are design rules (K = 0.2, 70 % proof; 0.5·π·d·Le shear area), not standard values. Not supplied: DIN 974-1 counterbore depths, ISO 10511 thin nyloc heights, SHCS shank tolerance, M2/M2.5 insert drills, fine-pitch threads.

**Doctrine.** Use this table for every metric hole the model draws: model a tapped hole at `minor_D1_mm`, drill it at `tap_drill_mm` (cut taps only — `sti_insert_drill_mm` for wire inserts, the tap maker's chart for form taps), clearance at `clearance_medium_mm` by default (fine for jig-drilled patterns, coarse for hand-drilled sheet), counterbore to `cbore_d_mm` (or knurled dk + 0.4 mm) and `cbore_depth_flush_mm`, hex pockets to `nut_ac_min_mm` across corners and `nut_h_mm` deep (`locknut_h_mm` if the joint is locked, which flight joints are), and check every tapped aluminium joint against `min_engagement_6061T6_8_8_mm` scaled per `design_rules.thread_engagement_length` — engagement length, not thread percentage, is what strips. The model must tell the user to measure when: the stock nut may be DIN 934 rather than ISO 4032 (M10/M12/M14 AF differ — check the bag before cutting a pocket); the head is knurled (use `shcs_head_d_knurled_mm`); the screw is countersunk or button (recall data — confirm from the vendor drawing); a stainless screw replaces a carbon-steel callout (A2-70 proof is 78 % of 8.8, 46 % of 12.9 — re-check preload); or the part is a pressure boundary, where every recall field must be confirmed against a vendor catalogue page (Bossard, Würth, McMaster ISO pages) before the first cut.

## 2. fasteners_un — Unified inch fasteners, #2-56 to 1/2-20

**File** `tables/fasteners_un.final.json` — 108,390 bytes, 16 rows (incl. added #12-24), 52 columns incl. a per-row `related_hardware` object.

**Sources.** ASME B1.1-2003 (basic profile, As formula and table, Appendix B shear area; FED-STD-H28); ASME B18.2.8-1999 Table 1 (clearance holes); ASME B18.3-2012 (SHCS head, keys, counterbore, button/flat head); ASME B94.11M-1993 (drill diameters); Machinery's Handbook 75 % tap-drill chart (convention); SAE J429-2014 (grades 5/8); ASTM A574, F837, F593 (materials, recall in part); ASME B18.2.1 / B18.2.2 / B18.6.3 / B18.22.1 (related_hardware, recall); NASA RP-1228 / MIL-HDBK-5 practice (shear = 0.6 UTS, K-factor torque). Cross-checked by script against gumyr/**cq_warehouse** CSVs fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com: `tap_hole_sizes.csv`, `clearance_hole_sizes.csv`, `socket_head_cap_parameters.csv`, `drill_sizes.csv` (Apache-2.0). Every tap drill, clearance drill, SHCS head diameter/height and key in the file matches those CSVs.

**Licence / attribution.** Standards facts (not copyrightable; documents copyright ASME/SAE/ASTM). cq_warehouse is Apache-2.0: the app must retain its copyright notice and licence reference where the data is credited. Attribution text:

> Unified-thread dimensions per ASME B1.1-2003, clearance holes per ASME B18.2.8-1999, socket-screw heads and counterbores per ASME B18.3-2012, drill diameters per ASME B94.11M-1993, strengths per SAE J429-2014 / ASTM A574 / F837 / F593. Compiled from memory and cross-checked against the cq_warehouse data files (gumyr/cq_warehouse, Apache License 2.0). Tap-drill choices are Machinery's Handbook conventions, not normative.

**Overall confidence:** likely (12 rows), recall (4 rows). No row certain: tap drills are conventions and clearance/SHCS values were checked against one open dataset, not the printed standard.

**Recall / disputed rows.** `#2-56` (clearance_close 3/32 vs #42 drill name; nominal 0.094 in agreed), `#12-24` (added; clearance from one dataset; SHCS/counterbore null — not a B18.3 size), `1/2-13` and `1/2-20` (clearance_loose 39/64 adopted; some charts show 5/8; compiler's 35/64 rejected). Recall fields: `clearance_close*` on 2 rows, `clearance_normal*` on 1, `clearance_loose*` on 3; **every `related_hardware` block** (hex cap screw AF/head height, hex and machine-screw nuts, Type A narrow washers, button-head and 82° flat-head dims, keys, countersink diameters) is recall and carries its own label. Notes (7) material values: A574 over-1/2-in yield 144 ksi and proof ~135–140 ksi are recall; F837/F593 stainless 100/65 ksi (1/4–5/8 in) and 70–90 ksi below 1/4 in are recall. `int_thread_shear_area_per_in_in2` is at basic, not 2B/2A minimum-material, dimensions (±10 %). Nylon shear-pin loads in notes (8) are recall ranges. Not supplied: low-head SHCS, STI drills, 2A/2B limits, dowel/press-fit tolerances, #2-64 / #4-48 / #6-40 / #8-36.

**Doctrine.** Treat this as the inch twin of fasteners_metric: tap at `tap_drill_75` in aluminium/brass/mild steel and at `tap_drill_50` in stainless, titanium or any hole deeper than 1.5D; clearance at `clearance_normal` unless the pattern is CNC-matched (close) or hand-drilled (loose); counterbore at `cbore_d` and `cbore_depth_min` + 0.010–0.030 in; size shear-pin holes in both parts at the close/normal value of the #2-56 / #4-40 / #6-32 row; and run the engagement check in notes (5) (1/4-20 A574 in 6061-T6 needs 1.8D) before any tapped aluminium joint carries thrust or pressure. The model must tell the user to measure or verify when: it is about to use anything from `related_hardware` (recall — read the vendor drawing of the actual nut, washer or button-head screw); the joint is a pressure boundary or motor-thrust path (spot-check the row against a printed Machinery's Handbook or the standard); a shear pin's load matters (pull-test the batch); or a stainless screw under 1/4 in is assumed to have 100 ksi UTS (it usually does not).

## 3. orings_as568 — SAE AS568 dash sizes with Parker ORD 5700 gland data

**File** `tables/orings_as568.final.json` — 356,309 bytes, 299 rows (−102…−178, −201…−284, −309…−395, −425…−475), 23 columns incl. per-row `parker_t42_in/mm` gland diameters; `supplement.gland_parker_ord5700` and `supplement.project_issues`.

**Sources.** SAE AS568D (2014) Table 1 — **not read directly**; compiler memory cross-checked by script against three independent open-source transcriptions on raw.githubusercontent.com: v2gundam/o-ring-fit `app/data/as568.generated.ts` (Apple Rubber AS568 quick-reference chart: ID, ID tol, W, W tol), bckasper3/KasperCalc `js/oring-sizes.js` (citing SAE AS568D), and the per-dash rows of KasperCalc `ORing-HighPressureIndustrial.html` (Parker O-Ring Handbook ORD 5700 Table 4-2) and `ORing-FaceSeal.html` (Chart 4-3). After the −151 correction all 299 id_in, cs_in and W tolerances agree across all three. Parker ORD 5700 Chart 4-1 (radial depth/squeeze), Figure 3-2 (extrusion), finish/chamfer and media guidance: recall of the printed handbook.

**Licence / attribution.** AS568 dimensions are published facts reproduced in every vendor catalogue; the standard is copyright SAE International. The gland recommendations are Parker Hannifin's published design data (ORD 5700, distributed free by Parker) — transcribed values, cited as Parker's; the club should hold its own copy of the handbook. The licences of v2gundam/o-ring-fit and bckasper3/KasperCalc were **not checked**; they were used only to cross-check standard facts and nothing of their code or expression is reproduced. Attribution text:

> O-ring dash sizes and tolerances per SAE AS568D (SAE International), cross-checked against published AS568 charts (Apple Rubber quick reference via v2gundam/o-ring-fit; KasperCalc). Gland design data per Parker Hannifin O-Ring Handbook ORD 5700 (Design Chart 4-1, Table 4-2, Chart 4-3, Figure 3-2), transcribed, not verified against the printed handbook where marked "recall". Selection practice marked "PartForge doctrine" is club practice, not Parker's.

**Overall confidence:** likely (289 rows), recall (10 rows), certain 0. Gland supplement: Chart 4-3 face-seal and Table 4-2 radial values likely; Chart 4-1 depth/squeeze, Figure 3-2 extrusion, finish/chamfer and media guidance recall.

**Recall / disputed rows.** `-151` (id corrected 2.925 → 2.987 in; three tables agree; kept recall by the lower-of-two rule), `-204` … `-209` (consistency skeptic proposed .351/.414/.468/.531/.594/.656; all three external tables support the kept .359/.421/.484/.546/.609/.671), `-225`, `-469`, `-470` (id_tol_in .018 vs .015, .095 vs .090 — larger carried). Parker Table 4-2 per-dash cells corrected from digit slips: `-130` A, `-348` A1, `-449` D; `-103`, `-141`, `-341` D-throat differ .001 in from the pattern and carry `parker_t42_note`. `supplement.gland_parker_ord5700`: Chart 4-1 depth L bands and squeeze %, Figure 3-2 curve points, finish/chamfer, radial "vacuum" widths (dropped as unplaceable) — recall. `supplement.project_issues` lists repo defects found: `tests/dryrun/endcap.scad` −240 ID 88.27 should be 94.84 mm and its radial groove is cut to the face-seal depth (groove bottom should be 95.96 mm, −0.05); `index.html` line 1394 doctrine face-seal widths are Table 4-2 radial widths mis-filed and its vacuum widths match neither chart; line 4397 hint prints no number; `embed.mjs` compact path drops tolerances and the supplement.

**Doctrine.** Select the dash by the sealing diameter, never by OD (`od_in` is derived, id + 2W, and is not a groove dimension): a face seal with pressure outside is sized to the O-ring ID, pressure inside to the OD, per `supplement.gland_parker_ord5700.usage_formulas`; a radial piston or rod gland uses the row's own `parker_t42_mm` (A bore, B1 piston groove, A1 rod groove, B rod, C plug, D throat) with the series' width G, tolerances and eccentricity from `radial_static_table_4_2`, and never the Chart 4-3 face-seal depth. Print the gland numbers at the moment of use (`cs_mm_std` for the cross-section, the depth/width band, the squeeze %), and state that Table 4-2 is a 1500 psi static rating. The model must tell the user to verify against the printed Parker handbook when the part is a pressure vessel (the 60 bar nitrous end cap): the extrusion limits, radial squeeze band and finish/chamfer figures are recall, and the difference between a face-seal and a radial groove depth on the same dash is exactly the error already in `endcap.scad`. It must also say that the four hand-copied Parker charts in the repo disagree and that this supplement is the one to read from.

## 4. npt — ASME B1.20.1 tapered pipe threads, 1/16 to 1 in

**File** `tables/npt.final.json` — 41,964 bytes, 7 rows, 66 columns incl. E0/E1/E2/K0, L1–L5, V, tap drills, boss and depth rules.

**Sources.** ASME B1.20.1-2013 Table 1 and its defining relations (E0 = D − (0.05D + 1.1)p, L2 = (0.8D + 6.8)p, L3 = 3p, V = 3.47p, L4 = L2 + V, L5 = L2 + L3, E1 = E0 + 0.0625 L1, E2 = E0 + 0.0625 L2, h = 0.8p, K0 = E0 − h, gauging ±1 turn) — recalled, re-derived, not verified against a copy. L4 for all seven sizes confirmed against BelfrySCAD/**BOSL2** `threading.scad` `npt_threaded_rod` info table (BSD-2-Clause; fetched 2026-09-17). Tap drills: Machinery's Handbook "Tap Drill Sizes for American National Standard Taper Pipe Threads" (unreamed and with-reamer columns; edition unverified). Boss OD, tapped depth, blind-drill depth, chamfer, fitting hex, metric nearest drills, port spacing, turns-past-finger-tight: shop rules of thumb, no standard.

**Licence / attribution.** Standards facts (document copyright ASME). BOSL2 is BSD-2-Clause and was used only as a cross-check. Attribution text:

> Taper pipe thread dimensions per ASME B1.20.1-2013 Table 1 and its formulas (re-derived; L4 cross-checked against BOSL2, BSD-2-Clause). Tap drills per Machinery's Handbook consensus. Boss diameter, tapped depth, drill depth, chamfer and fitting-hex figures are shop rules of thumb, not standard values and not pressure-vessel minimums.

**Overall confidence:** likely (all 7 rows). Certain fields: OD, tpi, pitch, taper, thread angle, h, H, L3, V (threads). Likely: L1, L2, L4, L5, V, E0–E2, K0, tap drills, gauge check. Recall: every rule-of-thumb column.

**Recall / disputed.** No recall row. Disputed: `npt_1-16.tap_drill` (D unreamed vs 1/4 on some charts; reamed C recall), `npt_1-8.tap_drill` (R vs 11/32; Q with reamer). Recall columns on all 7 rows: `tap_drill_alternatives`, `tap_drill_metric_nearest`, `min_tapped_depth_mm`, `min_boss_height_through_port_mm`, `blind_drill_depth_full_dia_mm`, `blind_drill_depth_to_point_mm`, `port_chamfer_dia_mm`, `typical_fitting_hex_af`, `min_boss_od_in/mm`, `min_boss_od_ratio_to_od`. Not supplied: a numeric floor thickness under a blind pocket port and any hoop-stress/burst figures.

**Doctrine.** Model an NPT port as a plain hole at `tap_drill` diameter (not at the nominal OD, and never a modelled taper) to `blind_drill_depth_full_dia_mm` for a blind port, attach a cosmetic thread so CAM and inspection read it as a thread, call it out as "x-yy NPT, gauge L1 plug flush ±1 turn", leave `min_tapped_depth_mm` (L2 + 2p) of material along the axis even at a through port, and keep `min_boss_od_mm` of solid material around it and `port_chamfer_dia_mm` of spot face for the fitting shoulder; prefer SAE J1926 / ISO 6149 straight-thread O-ring ports for pressure vessels. The model must tell the user, every time, that `min_boss_od` and the depth figures are rules of thumb and that the pressure-boundary calculation (hoop stress, wedging stress, burst margin) is outside the table; that the tap depth, not the drill, sets the port and must be gauged with an L1 plug or the actual fitting; that the L-values were recalled and must be verified against ASME B1.20.1 before a flight pressure part is cut; that BSPT/BSPP fittings are not NPT; and that oxidiser service needs oxygen-compatible PTFE, not a generic anaerobic sealant.

## 5. drills — twist-drill diameter lookup (number, letter, fractional, metric)

**File** `tables/drills.final.json` — 283,803 bytes, 282 rows (193 original + 89 added for sibling-table joins), 18 columns.

**Sources.** ASME B94.11M-1993 (fractional, number/wire-gauge and letter series); Machinery's Handbook "Standard Drill Sizes" and "Accuracy of Drilled Holes" (recall); ISO 235 / DIN 338 (metric jobber series; DIN 338 lengths recall); DIN 345 (Morse-taper sizes > 20 mm); ISO 286-2 (h8 deviations); the sibling tables fasteners_metric, fasteners_un and npt (their referenced drills define the added rows). The earlier "R'40 per ISO 3294" citation was wrong and is withdrawn.

**Licence / attribution.** Standards facts. Attribution text:

> Drill diameters per ASME B94.11M-1993 (inch series) and the ISO 235 / DIN 338 / DIN 345 stocked metric series; h8 deviations per ISO 286-2; jobber lengths per DIN 338 (recall). Nominal drill diameters only — a drilled hole cuts oversize.

**Overall confidence:** certain (204 rows), likely (74), recall (4). Every diameter is exact by definition; the likely/recall labels concern whether the size is a catalogue-stocked standard size (#80–#60 kept "likely" as honest; fractional 1-1/64…1-1/4 likely on series membership).

**Recall / disputed.** Recall rows: `mm_14_3`, `mm_17_9` (fasteners_metric stainless tap drills for M16/M20), `mm_14_75`, `mm_18_25` (npt nearest-metric) — on vendor availability. Disputed field `mm` on the ten odd-sixteenth tie rows `frac_1-16`, `frac_3-16`, `frac_5-16`, `frac_7-16`, `frac_9-16`, `frac_11-16`, `frac_13-16`, `frac_15-16`, `frac_1-1-16`, `frac_1-3-16`; the half-even value actually differs from half-up on five of them (3/16 = 4.762 vs 4.763, 7/16 = 11.112 vs 11.113, 11/16 = 17.462 vs 17.463, 15/16 = 23.812 vs 23.813, 1-3/16 = 30.162 vs 30.163) — 0.0005 mm, no machining consequence, but a value-join will miss. Recall on every row: `din338_jobber_oal_mm`, `din338_jobber_flute_mm`; `iso_h8_lower_dev_mm` numbers certain but applicability to all drills likely. Dowel/reamer prep pairs and the +0.02…+0.13 mm oversize rule in notes are recall. Not supplied: ASME inch jobber lengths and inch tolerance table, stub/long-series lengths.

**Doctrine.** Use this table only to turn a drill *name* into a diameter for display and for the "nearest stocked drill" question; the tap-drill and clearance-drill *assignments* live in fasteners_un, fasteners_metric and npt, and the join is on `name`. Display `in_text` / `mm_text`, not the numeric fields, so the machinist sees the standard's places (#78 = 0.0160). Never present a drill diameter as an as-machined hole size: for a press fit, dowel, O-ring gland bore or any pressure-bearing feature the model must say "drill undersize and ream or bore to size" and offer the dowel prep pairs as recall. The model must tell the user to check stock when it picks 14.3, 14.75, 17.9 or 18.25 mm, and to check the thread percentage before substituting the `nearest_alt_name` inch/metric neighbour (3.3 mm → #30 is 0.036 mm small for M4).

## 6. stock — 6061/6082 aluminium bar, plate, tube and pipe

**File** `tables/stock.final.json` — 383,512 bytes, 192 rows (bar 54, plate 25, tube 104, pipe 9), 42 columns.

**Sources.** ASTM B211 (cold-finished rod), B221 (extruded rod and tube), B209 (sheet/plate), B210 (drawn seamless tube), B241 (pipe, tolerance recall) with ASME B36.10M schedule dimensions (certain); EN 754-2 / 755-2 (metric bar), EN 485-3 / 485-4 (metric plate/sheet), EN 755-7 / 754-7 (metric tube — PROXY tolerances only); all as recall of Aluminum Standards & Data-era tables (2010s) and 2008–2016 EN editions. Availability: recall of McMaster-Carr, Online Metals, Metal Supermarkets and generic EU/UK stockists. Cast tooling plate strengths: Alcoa MIC-6 / Vista ATP-5 data sheets (recall). Spot-check skeptic independently agreed the B211, B209 and B210 OD bands.

**Licence / attribution.** Supplier-catalogue reality plus paraphrased standards tables. Standards text copyright ASTM / CEN, not reproduced. Attribution text:

> Stock sizes and tolerances paraphrased from recall of ASTM B211 / B221 / B209 / B210 / B241 and EN 754-2 / 755-2 / 485-3 / 485-4 (editions unverified); pipe dimensions per ASME B36.10M; metric tube tolerances are deliberately wide proxies, not EN 755-7 values; availability is catalogue recall (McMaster-Carr, Online Metals, Metal Supermarkets, EU/UK stockists). Nothing here is a finished dimension.

**Overall confidence:** recall (148 rows), likely (44 rows), certain 0. Geometry and derived numbers (dim_min/max, max_finish, id_min/max, clears_casing, mass) are exact arithmetic on recalled tolerances.

**Recall / disputed.** The 44 likely rows are: `bar_6061_i_0.250/0.375/0.500/0.625/0.750/1.000/1.250/1.500/2.000/2.500/3.000`; `plate_6061_i_0.125/0.1875/0.250/0.375/0.500/0.625/0.750/1.000/1.500/2.000`; `tube_6061_i_1.500x0.049/0.065/0.083/0.125/0.250`, `1.750x0.065/0.125`, `2.000x0.049/0.065/0.083/0.125/0.250`, `2.500x0.065/0.125/0.250`, `3.000x0.065/0.125/0.250`, `3.500x0.125/0.250`, `4.000x0.065/0.125/0.250`. **Every other row is recall**: all `bar_6061_m_*` and `plate_6061_m_*`, all `_ext` / `_drawn` twins, all intermediate inch bars (5/16, 7/16, 7/8, 1-3/4, 2-1/4, 2-3/4) and bars 3.5–8.0 in, plate 5/16 and 1-1/4, every tube not in the list above, all nine `pipe_6061_i_*`, all fifteen `tube_6082_m_*` (proxy tolerances, `tolerance_basis` = `en_755_7_proxy`). ## What to tell the user before any of this cuts metal

1. **Nothing here was read from a standard or a vendor drawing.** Every table was compiled from memory, reviewed by three more memories, and cross-checked only where an open-source copy happened to exist on GitHub. Three recalls agreeing is not verification. "certain" is reserved for values fixed by definition or arithmetic; every other label is an honest estimate of how likely the number is to match the printed source, and the printed source still has to be opened before a flight part is cut.

2. **The pressure-vessel path is the least verified path.** The 60 bar nitrous end cap touches four tables and every one of them has a recall link in the chain: NPT boss diameter and depth are shop rules of thumb with no hoop-stress check; the O-ring radial squeeze band, extrusion limits and finish are recall of the Parker handbook; stock wall tolerance and property minima are recall of ASTM/EN tables; fastener engagement and torque are design rules, and stainless substitution silently loses up to 54 % of proof load. None of these tables contains a burst margin. That calculation must be done separately, on measured stock, and reviewed by a person.

3. **The repo already contains the kind of error these tables exist to prevent.** `tests/dryrun/endcap.scad` carries the wrong mm ID for AS568-240 (88.27, should be 94.84) and cuts a radial groove to the face-seal depth; `index.html` line 1394 quotes radial groove widths as face-seal widths and vacuum widths that match neither chart; four hand-copied Parker charts disagree with each other. Fix these before the tables go live, or the hints will be contradicted by the doctrine line beside them.

4. **Every dimension that mates with bought hardware must be measured.** Airframe and coupler IDs/ODs are volunteer transcriptions of catalogue nominals and contain rows with zero or negative clearance and rows the database itself marks as wrong; motor case ODs and closure geometry are recall with unresolved contradictions; no altimeter hole pattern is verified; every rail-slot internal is recall; commercial rail buttons come from a community file with known-bad entries. The model should say "measure it" for these every time, and the template should carry a measured-override field and a test-ring step.

5. **Motor retention cannot be designed from the motors table.** It holds loaded length and mass only; the closure architecture of CTI Pro-X is recorded as three contradictory accounts. Look at the club's own case.

6. **Rounding conventions differ.** `drills` rounds ties half-to-even (ASME printed column); the other tables round half-up. 3/16 in is 4.762 in one and 4.763 in the others. Join on names. The current `dbRows()` recomputation `+(inch*25.4).toFixed(3)` produces 1.587 for 1/16, 14.287 for 9/16 and 20.637 for 13/16 — values matching neither convention — so mm must be embedded, not recomputed.

7. **Fastener strength traps that the tables document but cannot enforce:** A2-70 stainless vs 8.8/10.9/12.9 callouts; stainless-on-stainless galling; electroplated 12.9 hydrogen embrittlement; stainless screws under 1/4 in are 70–90 ksi, not 100; #2–#12 machine screws carry no J429 grade; thread percentage above ~65 % adds nothing, engagement length in aluminium is what strips.

8. **Known-bad rows were kept on purpose** (so a query can explain why a number is wrong) and are flagged in `disputed`; a hint must never print such a row without its flag. The do-not-use lists in `motors` and `airframes` notes are the minimum.

9. **Vintage.** Vendor data is 2014–2022; Giant Leap phenolic is mostly discontinued, Gorilla is defunct, AMW is back, Raven4 is superseded by Blue Raven, Madcow FT75/80/115 are custom order, standard editions are unverified. Availability is recall.

10. **Scope gaps a designer may not expect:** no fine-pitch metric threads; no 2A/2B limits; no dowel/press-fit tolerance table; no low-head SHCS; no STI drills for inch; no 304 stainless stock table; no arming-switch or battery-holder envelopes; no V-slot or 4040 rails; no floor-thickness or burst numbers for ports; AS568 −0xx and −9xx sizes are not in the table.
